Across Australia

Where within Australia are threatened animals found, and how serious is their status?

Threatened species are present in every state and territory.

All states and territories have species listed as threatened under the EPBC Act, the federal law that lists nationally threatened species, but the numbers vary. New South Wales has far more threatened species than South Australia or the Northern Territory, even though they cover similar or larger areas.

How can we monitor effectively?

Monitoring biodiversity is harder than you think.

Notice a trend in the graphs above? Monitoring population health is very difficult, for a number of reasons. Illustrated above is a form of sampling bias, where the outcome of the experiment is biased by how the experiment was sampled. In this case, on the left you can see how the Regent Honeyeater population appears to increase over time, even though we know their population is dwindling and their range is contracting. On the right, we can see why: the number of studies measuring threatened species is increasing drastically over time. This trend is especially notable for birds.

The Threatened Species Index (TSX) was designed to solve this problem and make biodiversity monitoring easier for scientists and policymakers. It combines studies and accounts for the higher sampling volume in later years, as well as other statistical factors such as the margin of error in each study.

The TSX is just one of many tools for tracking biodiversity.

Ecologists all over Australia are innovating to find new ways to track biodiversity and help improve Australia's conservation outcomes.
