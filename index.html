Australia in the world

Which countries have the most threatened species, and where does Australia stand?

Threatened species are present all over the globe. Conservation International recognises a set of countries as "megadiverse," highlighted in the map above. It means they meet a set of criteria that leads to much higher biodiversity compared to other countries. Of course, higher biodiversity means more species that could go extinct. Australia is a megadiverse country, so how does it size up to the others?

02

Across Australia

Where within Australia are threatened animals found, and how serious is their status?

Threatened species are present in every state and territory.

While some states manage greater numbers of threatened species, all states and territories have species listed as threatened under the Environmental Protection and Biodiversity Conservation (EPBC) Act, a federal act describing national threatened species. However, some states have greater numbers than others. New South Wales has a much higher amount of threatened species compared to regions with similar or even greater area, like South Australia or the Northern Territory.

How can we monitor effectively?

Monitoring biodiversity is harder than you think.

Notice a trend in the graphs above? Monitoring population health is very difficult, for a number of reasons. Illustrated above is a form of sampling bias, where the outcome of the experiment is biased by how the experiment was sampled. In this case, on the left you can see how the Regent Honeyeater population appears to increase over time, even though we know their population is dwindling and their range is contracting. On the right, we can see why: the number of studies measuring threatened species is increasing drastically over time. This trend is especially notable for birds.

Introducing the TSX, or Threatened Species Index, an index that aims to solve this problem and make biodiversity monitoring easier for scientists and policymakers. It aggregates studies together, and accounts for higher sampling volume in later years, as well as many other complicated statistical calculations like margin of error on each individual study.
